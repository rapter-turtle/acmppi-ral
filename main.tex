%%  ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
Terrain-referenced navigation (TRN) allows autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) to bound dead-reckoning drift by matching altimeter measurements to a bathymetric map, but its reliability depends strongly on the path taken. When the initial uncertainty is large, repetitive or elongated seafloor features such as sand waves and stripes produce several equally plausible position hypotheses, and the filter may converge to a wrong one. Common terrain information measures, namely the Fisher information of the terrain gradient, the elevation standard deviation, and the terrain entropy, are local or magnitude-based and therefore overconfident on such terrain. This paper presents an ambiguity-aware active localization framework that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into model predictive path integral (MPPI) control. The model evaluates the local terrain autocorrelation under the current belief size, retains only the information that remains unambiguous, and reduces to the Fisher information in the small-uncertainty limit. Precomputed at multiple belief scales, it is embedded in a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model. In 8,100 Monte Carlo runs with a six-degree-of-freedom REMUS~100 model on ten real bathymetric maps and three initial uncertainties, the proposed planner achieved a success rate of 86.3\% and the highest rate at every uncertainty level, compared with 75.1--83.6\% for A* and MPPI planners using the Fisher, standard-deviation, or entropy maps and 55.2\% for straight-line guidance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} underwater vehicles (AUVs) operate without access to the global navigation satellite system (GNSS), and their dead-reckoning (DR) solution obtained from inertial sensors and a Doppler velocity log (DVL) drifts without bound~\cite{paull2014auv}. Terrain-referenced navigation (TRN) bounds this drift by matching onboard seafloor measurements to a prior bathymetric map~\cite{bergman1999terrain, carreno2010survey, melo2017survey}, even with a single-beam altimeter that most AUVs already carry~\cite{kim2014nonlinear, claus2015terrain, salavasidis2019terrain}, and thereby removes the need to surface for GNSS fixes or to deploy acoustic beacons (Fig.~\ref{fig:auv}).

However, the performance of TRN depends on the terrain the vehicle traverses, and reliable TRN remains challenging due to several coupled factors. First, the initial position uncertainty after a long DR transit can reach tens of meters, which is comparable to the characteristic length of many seafloor features. Second, a single-beam altimeter provides one scalar measurement per ping, so the observable position components depend on the direction in which the terrain is crossed. Third, natural seafloors frequently contain repetitive or elongated features such as sand waves, linear stripes, and ridges (Fig.~\ref{fig:terrains}), on which several position hypotheses explain the measurements equally well. The particle filter (PF) can then converge to a wrong hypothesis while reporting a small covariance, resulting in a large undetected error at the destination. Therefore, the vehicle must actively select a path that provides information that is not only abundant but also unambiguous at its current level of uncertainty.

Information-aware planners quantify the information of the terrain with measures such as the Fisher information of the terrain gradient, the elevation standard deviation, or the terrain entropy~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, wang2015construction, prentice2009belief, hausler2013cooperative}. These measures are local or magnitude-based: they are accurate once the belief is narrower than the terrain features, but they are overconfident when the uncertainty is large, because steep, rough, or repetitive terrain yields large values even though its features cannot be told apart (Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}). Particle-based information measures avoid this approximation~\cite{hoffmann2010mobile, ryan2010particle}, but their cost grows with the number of particles and simulated measurements, which is prohibitive when hundreds of candidate trajectories must be evaluated at every replanning step.

Motivated by these observations, this paper proposes an ambiguity-aware active localization framework that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into model predictive path integral (MPPI) control~\cite{williams2017model, williams2018information}. The model combines the local terrain autocorrelation with the current belief size and retains only the information that remains unambiguous. Because it depends only on the map and the belief size, it is precomputed at several belief scales and looked up at the cost of a Fisher information map. The MPPI planner embeds the model in a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model, so that information from repetitive features is counted only to the extent that the current belief can resolve them.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An autocorrelation-based terrain information model is proposed, which quantifies the information that remains unambiguous at the current belief size and reduces to the Fisher information in the small-uncertainty limit.
    \item An MPPI-based active localization framework is developed that embeds the information model into a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model, using multi-scale precomputed information maps for real-time planning.
    \item The framework is validated through 8,100 Monte Carlo runs with a six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) REMUS~100 model on ten real bathymetric maps and three initial uncertainties, against straight-line guidance and A* and MPPI planners that use the Fisher, standard-deviation, and entropy maps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
TRN estimates the vehicle position by comparing measured depths or altitudes with a bathymetric map~\cite{bergman1999terrain, nygren2004terrain, melo2017survey}, typically with PFs because the posterior is frequently multimodal~\cite{arulampalam2002tutorial, donovan2012position, teixeira2017robust, kim2018comparison}, and it has been demonstrated with multibeam sonars as well as low-cost altimeters on long-range AUVs and gliders~\cite{meduna2008low, claus2015terrain, salavasidis2019terrain}. These studies treat the path as given and report that the accuracy depends on the terrain traversed~\cite{nygren2004terrain, melo2017survey}. The suitability of terrain for TRN is commonly assessed by the elevation standard deviation, the terrain entropy, and the Fisher information~\cite{wang2015construction}, and the posterior Cram\'{e}r--Rao lower bound (PCRLB), i.e., the inverse of the accumulated Fisher information of the terrain gradient, is widely used to predict the accuracy~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, bergman1999terrain}. These measures characterize the error around the true position, but not the large errors caused by competing hypotheses. Such errors belong to the threshold region, where global bounds such as the Ziv--Zakai bound are governed by the ambiguity of the measurement under a parameter offset rather than by its local curvature~\cite{ziv1969some}. In TRN, this ambiguity is determined by the terrain autocorrelation, which also underlies correlation-based terrain matching~\cite{nygren2004terrain}.

Active localization selects actions that reduce the localization uncertainty~\cite{fox1998active}. Belief-space planners propagate a Gaussian belief with the Fisher information or an extended Kalman filter~\cite{prentice2009belief, vandenberg2011lqg, bry2011rapidly, indelman2015planning}, and perception-aware methods embed Fisher-based localization costs into planning or receding-horizon control~\cite{zhang2019beyond, falanga2018pampc}. For TRN, planners steer the vehicle over terrain with high information content~\cite{hausler2013cooperative}, optimize waypoints for the Monte Carlo probability of reaching the goal~\cite{krukowski2016waypoint}, or select actions that discriminate between PF hypotheses~\cite{fairfield2008active}. Fisher-based predictions inherit the local nature of the Fisher information, whereas particle-based and Monte Carlo evaluations~\cite{hoffmann2010mobile, ryan2010particle, krukowski2016waypoint} are costly inside a sampling-based planner. MPPI control~\cite{williams2016aggressive, williams2017model, williams2018information} optimizes nonsmooth costs over sampled rollouts of nonlinear dynamics and suits active localization if the information cost of each rollout can be evaluated in real time. The proposed framework brings these aspects together: an ambiguity-aware information model that is as inexpensive to evaluate as a Fisher information map, embedded in an MPPI planner with AUV dynamics.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{AUV Model}
The AUV is described by a 6-DOF rigid-body model:
\begin{align}
  \dot{\eta} &= J_\Theta(\eta)\,\nu, \label{eq:kinematics} \\
  M\dot{\nu} + C(\nu)\nu + D(\nu)\nu + g(\eta) &= \tau(\delta_r, \delta_s, n), \label{eq:kinetics}
\end{align}
where $\eta = [x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi]^\top$ is the position and attitude, $\nu = [u, v, w, p, q, r]^\top$ is the body-fixed velocity, $J_\Theta$ is the transformation matrix, $M$ is the inertia matrix including the added mass, $C$ is the Coriolis--centripetal matrix, $D$ is the damping including the cross-flow drag and body lift, $g$ is the restoring force, and $\tau$ is generated by the rudder angle $\delta_r$, stern-plane angle $\delta_s$, and propeller speed $n$. The REMUS~100 parameters~\cite{allen2000propulsion} of an open-source implementation\footnote{\url{https://github.com/cybergalactic/PythonVehicleSimulator}} are used. The vehicle cruises at a constant propeller speed, follows the seafloor at a constant altitude with a depth autopilot, and is steered by a heading autopilot.

\subsection{Terrain-Referenced Navigation}
Let $\bp_k = [x_k, y_k]^\top$ be the horizontal position at the $k$-th altimeter ping with period $T_p$. With the heading and attitude measured, the DVL-based DR provides the displacement $\Delta\bp_k = \bp_{k+1} - \bp_k$ as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\hat{\bp}_k = \Delta\bp_k + \mathbf{w}_k, \qquad \mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_w^2 I),
  \label{eq:dr}
\end{equation}
and the altimeter measures the seafloor elevation $h(\cdot)$ of the prior map at its beam footprint,
\begin{equation}
  z_k = h(\bp_k + \bo_k) + v_k, \qquad v_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2),
  \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
where $\bo_k$ is the footprint offset determined from the attitude and the measured range. A PF with particles $\{\bx_k^i, \omega_k^i\}_{i=1}^{N_p}$~\cite{arulampalam2002tutorial} propagates each particle by $\bx_{k}^i = \bx_{k-1}^i + \Delta\hat{\bp}_{k-1} + \mathbf{q}^i$, $\mathbf{q}^i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_q^2 I)$, and reweights it as
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k^i \propto \omega_{k-1}^i \exp\!\Big( -\beta_k \frac{(z_k - h(\bx_k^i + \bo_k))^2}{2\sigma_L^2} \Big),
  \label{eq:pf}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_L > \sigma_z$ is inflated against weight degeneracy and $\beta_k \in (0,1]$ is the largest tempering exponent that keeps the effective sample size (ESS) above $0.3N_p$. Systematic resampling with roughening is applied when the ESS falls below $0.5N_p$. The PF provides the estimate $\hat{\bx}_k$ and the covariance $P_k$ of the particles.

\subsection{Active Localization Problem}
The vehicle starts at $\bp_0 = \bar{\bp}_0 + \mathbf{e}_0$, $\mathbf{e}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_0^2 I)$, around the nominal start $\bar{\bp}_0$ at which the PF is initialized with the same $\sigma_0$, and must reach the goal $\bg$. The mission terminates when $\|\bg - \hat{\bx}_k\| \le r_a$. It is successful if $\|\bp_k - \hat{\bx}_k\| \le r_a$ at that moment, i.e., the vehicle has actually reached the goal, a false arrival otherwise, and a timeout if no arrival occurs within $T_{\max}$. The planner seeks heading-rate commands that minimize
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\big[\|\bp_{k_f} - \hat{\bx}_{k_f}\|^2\big] + \alpha\, T_f,
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $k_f$ and $T_f$ are the arrival ping and time and $\alpha$ weights the travel time. Since the belief is multimodal, the expectation is approximated by the trace of the predicted covariance at arrival, which requires predicting how the belief contracts along a candidate path (Section~\ref{sec:info}).

\section{Autocorrelation-Based Terrain Information}
\label{sec:info}

\subsection{Limitation of Local Terrain Information Measures}
For the measurement~\eqref{eq:measurement} with the likelihood standard deviation $\sigma_L$, the Fisher information on the position from a single ping at $\bp$ is
\begin{equation}
  \JF(\bp) = \sigma_L^{-2}\,\nabla h(\bp)\,\nabla h(\bp)^\top,
  \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
and the covariance along a path $\bp_1, \ldots, \bp_T$ is predicted by
\begin{equation}
  P_t = \big( (P_{t-1} + \sigma_q^2 I)^{-1} + J(\bp_t) \big)^{-1}
  \label{eq:riccati}
\end{equation}
with $J = \JF$, which yields the PCRLB~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, bergman1999terrain}. This prediction is valid only while the error remains in the region where $h$ is approximately linear. On repetitive terrain, positions offset by one period produce identical measurements; if the uncertainty exceeds the period, \eqref{eq:riccati} with $J = \JF$ predicts a contraction that no Bayesian filter can realize (Remark~\ref{rem:periodic}), and a planner relying on it is attracted to precisely the terrain that causes false convergence. Magnitude-based measures such as the elevation standard deviation and the terrain entropy~\cite{wang2015construction} share this limitation and, in addition, ignore the direction in which the terrain is informative.

\subsection{Local Autocorrelation and the Offset Posterior}
Consider the expected squared measurement residual when the belief is offset from the truth by $\bd$ while the vehicle crosses a window $\mathcal{W}(\bx)$ of side $w$ centered at $\bx$:
\begin{equation}
  D(\bx, \bd) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\int_{\mathcal{W}(\bx)} \big( h(\bq + \bd) - h(\bq) \big)^2 \, d\bq.
  \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
For locally stationary terrain, $D(\bx,\bd) = 2\big(R_{\bx}(\mathbf{0}) - R_{\bx}(\bd)\big)$ with the local autocorrelation $R_{\bx}$. $D$ vanishes at $\bd = \mathbf{0}$, and a small value at $\bd \neq \mathbf{0}$ indicates an offset that the terrain cannot distinguish from the truth. If $M$ pings are collected within the window while the belief is an isotropic Gaussian of size $\sigma$, the posterior over the offset is
\begin{equation}
  \pi_\sigma(\bd \mid \bx) \propto \exp\!\Big( -\frac{\|\bd\|^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{M\,D(\bx,\bd)}{2\sigma_A^2} \Big),
  \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_A$ is the residual scale of the model, a design parameter that need not equal $\sigma_L$. Its second moment about the true offset,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_\sigma(\bx) = \textstyle\int \bd\,\bd^\top\, \pi_\sigma(\bd \mid \bx)\, d\bd,
  \label{eq:second_moment}
\end{equation}
is the expected squared error after crossing the window, and the autocorrelation-based information per ping is defined as the corresponding increase of the precision,
\begin{equation}
  \JA(\bx; \sigma) = M^{-1}\big[\, \Sigma_\sigma(\bx)^{-1} - \sigma^{-2} I \,\big]_+,
  \label{eq:Jac}
\end{equation}
where $[A]_+ = V\max(\Lambda, 0)V^\top$ for $A = V\Lambda V^\top$. Because $\Sigma_\sigma$ is taken about the true offset rather than the posterior mean, probability mass on a competing offset increases $\Sigma_\sigma$ and reduces $\JA$ in the direction of the ambiguity. Fig.~\ref{fig:maps} compares the expected error $E_{\mathrm{AC}} = \sqrt{\tr\Sigma_{\sigma_0}}$ with the corresponding errors predicted by the Fisher, standard-deviation, and entropy measures: the latter rate stripes, slopes, and sand waves as informative, whereas the autocorrelation retains only the few locations that are unambiguous at $\sigma_0 = 50$~m.

\subsection{Properties}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fisher}
Let $h$ be four times continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of $\mathcal{W}(\bx)$, and let $G(\bx) = |\mathcal{W}|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{W}(\bx)} \nabla h\,\nabla h^\top d\bq$. Then, for a fixed $M$, $\JA(\bx;\sigma) = \sigma_A^{-2}\,G(\bx) + O(\sigma^2)$ as $\sigma \to 0$, i.e., $\JA$ converges to the window-averaged Fisher information with the residual scale $\sigma_A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Taylor expansion, $D(\bx,\bd) = \bd^\top G\bd + c_3(\bd) + O(\|\bd\|^4)$ with a homogeneous cubic form $c_3$. Without the higher-order terms, \eqref{eq:posterior} is a zero-mean Gaussian with precision $A = \sigma^{-2}I + (M/\sigma_A^2)G$, and \eqref{eq:Jac} gives exactly $G/\sigma_A^2$. Since $\pi_\sigma$ concentrates on $\|\bd\| = O(\sigma)$, the first-order perturbation of the second moment by the odd form $c_3$ vanishes, and the remaining perturbations are $\delta\Sigma = O(M\sigma^6) + O(M^2\sigma^8)$. With $\|A\| = O(\sigma^{-2})$, $\Sigma_\sigma^{-1} = A - A\,\delta\Sigma\,A + \ldots = A + O(M\sigma^2)$. Since $G \succeq 0$, the projection $[\cdot]_+$ changes the result by at most the same order, and dividing by $M$ yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:periodic}
For a sand-wave field $h(\bq) = a\cos(2\pi\,\mathbf{n}^\top\bq/\lambda)$ with crest normal $\mathbf{n}$ and a window spanning several wavelengths, the window-averaged Fisher information $(2\pi a/\lambda)^2/(2\sigma_L^2)\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^\top$ is independent of $\sigma$, whereas $D(\bx,\bd) = a^2\big(1 - \cos(2\pi\,\mathbf{n}^\top\bd/\lambda)\big)$ vanishes at every $\bd = m\lambda\mathbf{n}$. If $\sigma \gg \lambda$, $\pi_\sigma$ consists of narrow peaks at $m\lambda\mathbf{n}$ weighted by the prior, so that $\Sigma_\sigma \approx \sigma^2 I$ and $\JA \approx 0$; if $\sigma \ll \lambda$, $\JA$ approaches the Fisher information by Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher}. Repetitive terrain is thus treated as uninformative until the belief can resolve its period, which is the behavior required for reliable TRN.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Precomputation and Normalization}
Because~\eqref{eq:D}--\eqref{eq:Jac} depend only on the map and $\sigma$, $\JA$ is precomputed offline on map nodes $\bx$ and a finite set of belief levels $\mathcal{S}$ between $\sigma_{\min}$ and $\sigma_{\max}$. For each offset $\bd$ on a square grid with spacing $\Delta_d = \max(\Delta_h, \sigma/5)$ within $\|\bd\| \le 3\sigma$, where $\Delta_h$ is the map resolution, the map $(h(\cdot + \bd) - h(\cdot))^2$ is box-filtered over the window, which yields $D(\cdot,\bd)$ for all nodes at once. The moments~\eqref{eq:second_moment} are accumulated with a streaming log-sum-exp, and $\Delta_d^2/12$ is added to the diagonal to account for the offset-grid resolution. The cost is $O(N_d N_h)$ per level for $N_d$ offsets and $N_h$ map cells. Since $\sigma_A$ and the offset-grid resolution scale $\JA$ as a whole, $\JA$ is multiplied by one factor per terrain such that its terrain mean at the smallest level $\sigma_{\min}$ equals that of the window-averaged Fisher information; this preserves the decrease of $\JA$ with $\sigma$ and gives all information maps compared in Section~\ref{sec:results} the same mean information. At run time, $\JA(\bx;\sigma)$ is obtained by a nearest-node lookup and a linear interpolation in $\log\sigma$, with $\sigma$ clipped to $[\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}]$. In this study, $\Delta_h = 5$~m, $w = 50$~m, $M = 33$ (the pings over $50$~m at $1.5$~m/s and $1$~Hz), $\sigma_A = 0.3$~m, the nodes are spaced by $20$~m, and $\mathcal{S} = \{50, 35, 20, 10, 5\}$~m, extended by $\sigma_0$ when $\sigma_0 > 50$~m.

\section{Autocorrelation-Informed MPPI}
\label{sec:mppi}
The proposed planner, referred to as the autocorrelation-informed MPPI (AC-MPPI), is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. At every replanning instant, it samples heading-rate sequences, rolls them out with an AUV maneuvering model from the PF estimate, predicts the covariance at arrival using $\JA$, and passes the optimized command to the heading autopilot.

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  % Generated by scripts/ral_figures.py fig4.
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig4_framework.pdf}
  \vspace{-14pt}
  \caption{Overall framework. The autocorrelation information maps are precomputed offline from the bathymetric map at multiple belief levels. At each replanning instant, AC-MPPI evaluates sampled heading-rate sequences by rolling out the AUV maneuvering model and predicting the covariance at arrival with the belief-dependent information.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
  \vspace{-12pt}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Rollout Model}
For the rollouts, \eqref{eq:kinetics} is reduced to the horizontal plane ($w = p = q = 0$, $\phi = \theta = 0$, fixed propeller speed), which yields the surge--sway--yaw model
\begin{equation}
  M_h\dot{\nu}_h + C_h(\nu_h)\nu_h + D_h(\nu_h)\nu_h = \tau_h(\delta_r)
  \label{eq:horizontal}
\end{equation}
with $\nu_h = [u, v, r]^\top$ and the corresponding rows and columns of~\eqref{eq:kinetics}. The decision variable is a heading-rate sequence $U = (u_0, \ldots, u_{N-1})$, $|u_n| \le r_{\max}$, each held for $\Delta t$, which the heading autopilot tracks as $\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) = \psi_0 + \int_0^t u\,dt'$ and $r_{\mathrm{ref}} = u$. Each rollout is integrated with a $0.5$~s step and evaluated at its step points $\bp_1, \ldots, \bp_T$ with durations $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_T$. A rollout that passes within $r_a$ of the goal is terminated there; otherwise, the path is completed by a straight segment to the goal, so that every candidate is evaluated up to arrival.

\subsection{Belief-Dependent Covariance Prediction}
Along each rollout, the covariance is predicted from $P_0 = P$ with $n_t = \tau_t / T_p$ pings per segment as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:prediction}
\begin{align}
  P_{t|t-1} &= P_{t-1} + n_t\,\sigma_q^2 I, \qquad \sigma_t = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\tr P_{t|t-1}}, \label{eq:pred_a} \\
  \bar{J}_t &= \textstyle\sum_{j} c_j\, \JA(\bp_t + \bs_j;\, \sigma_t), \label{eq:pred_c} \\
  P_t &= \big( P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + n_t\,\bar{J}_t \big)^{-1}, \label{eq:pred_d}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\bs_0 = \mathbf{0}$ and $\bs_{\pm i} = \pm\sqrt{3\mu_i}\,\mathbf{e}_i$ are the sigma points of $P_0$ with eigenpairs $(\mu_i, \mathbf{e}_i)$, $c_0 = 1/3$, and $c_{\pm i} = 1/6$. The sigma points average the information over the region where the vehicle may actually be, so that a narrow feature that an offset vehicle would miss does not attract the planner. The belief level $\sigma_t$ is taken from the predicted covariance itself, so repetitive terrain contributes to~\eqref{eq:pred_d} only after the predicted belief has been narrowed by unambiguous terrain earlier along the same rollout. The cost of a sequence $U$ is
\begin{equation}
  S(U) = \tr P_T + \alpha\, T(U) + c_{\mathrm{out}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{out}}(U),
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $T(U) = \sum_t \tau_t$ and $\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{out}}$ penalizes rollouts that leave the map. Replacing $\JA$ in~\eqref{eq:pred_c} by another information map yields the baseline MPPI planners of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{MPPI Update}
At each replanning instant, $K$ sequences $U^k = \mathrm{clip}(\bar{U} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k)$, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_u^2 I)$, are sampled around the nominal sequence $\bar{U}$, which is included unperturbed together with the direct-to-goal sequence $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$. With the weights
\begin{equation}
  \rho^k \propto \exp\!\big(-(S(U^k) - S_{\min})/\gamma\big), \qquad \textstyle\sum_k \rho^k = 1,
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
whose temperature $\gamma$ is set by bisection so that the ESS $(\sum_k (\rho^k)^2)^{-1}$ equals $0.1K$, the nominal sequence is updated as
\begin{equation}
  \bar{U} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\Big(\bar{U} + \textstyle\sum_{k}\rho^k\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k\Big),
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
and the cheaper of $\bar{U}$ and $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$ is executed. Its first command is applied as $r_{\mathrm{ref}} = u_0$ and $\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) = \psi(t_r) + u_0(t - t_r)$ until the next replanning at $t_r + T_r$, and $\bar{U}$ is shifted by one step as a warm start (Algorithm~\ref{alg:acmppi}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{AC-MPPI at a replanning instant}
\label{alg:acmppi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\hat{\bx}$, $P$, $\psi$, $\nu_h$, $\bg$, $\bar{U}$, precomputed $\JA(\cdot;\sigma)$, $\sigma \in \mathcal{S}$
\STATE Sample $U^k$, $k = 1,\ldots,K$; include $\bar{U}$ and $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$
\FOR{$k = 1$ \TO $K$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \STATE Roll out~\eqref{eq:horizontal} under $U^k$; append the segment to $\bg$
  \STATE Predict $P_T$ by~\eqref{eq:prediction}; evaluate $S(U^k)$ by~\eqref{eq:cost}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Set $\gamma$ for ESS $= 0.1K$; update $\bar{U}$ by~\eqref{eq:weights}--\eqref{eq:update}
\STATE \textbf{if} $S(U_{\mathrm{dir}}) \le S(\bar{U})$ \textbf{then} $\bar{U} \leftarrow U_{\mathrm{dir}}$
\STATE Apply $u_0$ for $T_r$; shift $\bar{U}$ by one step
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Terrains and routes}
Ten $2$~km~$\times$~$2$~km crops of the NOAA BlueTopo bathymetry\footnote{\url{https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/data/bluetopo.html}} covered by full-coverage multibeam surveys were resampled to $5$~m (Fig.~\ref{fig:terrains}). One route per terrain was defined by a fixed rule: a length of $1.4$~km, a heading within $\pm 10^\circ$ of the dominant feature direction obtained from the terrain structure tensor, and the straight corridor with the largest mean expected error after one pass, subject to an informative area within $300$~m. The terrains were selected from the maps and the difficulty of straight-line guidance only, not from the results of the compared planners: S1--S6 because the route-mean expected error of the autocorrelation map exceeds that of the Fisher map by a factor of at least $1.7$, and S7--S10 because it exceeds that of the standard-deviation map by a factor of at least $2$, in both cases with a straight-line success rate of at most $70\%$ in preliminary runs. The routes thus represent missions in which the direct path runs along ambiguous structure while unambiguous terrain is reachable, which favors information-aware methods over straight-line guidance by design. S6 and S9 show survey-line striping, and S1 lies deeper than the rating of the REMUS~100; since the same map serves as the true seafloor and the navigation map, these affect realism but not the fairness of the comparison.

\subsubsection{Compared methods}
Nine methods were compared: Straight-LOS, i.e., line-of-sight (LOS) guidance to the goal ($80$~m lookahead, straight final approach within $150$~m, go-around after a missed arrival), and the combinations of two planners with four information maps. The \emph{Fisher} map uses~\eqref{eq:fisher}. The \emph{Std} and \emph{Entropy} maps use the elevation variance $s_{\mathrm{S}}(\bx)$ in the window and the terrain entropy measure $s_{\mathrm{E}}(\bx) = \log N - H_f$, $H_f = -\sum_{ij} p_{ij}\log p_{ij}$ with $p_{ij} = d_{ij}/\sum d_{ij}$ over the depths $d_{ij}$ of the $N$ cells in the window~\cite{wang2015construction}, cast into isotropic information $J_m = c_m s_m(\bx) I$. The \emph{AC} map uses~\eqref{eq:Jac}. All maps are scaled per terrain to the same terrain-mean information as the window-averaged Fisher information, so that they differ only in how the information is distributed. The A* planners plan once from the initial estimate on an $8$-connected $20$~m grid with the edge cost (length)$\times(1 + \lambda u)$, where $u \in [0,1]$ is the normalized expected error after one pass, $E_m = \sqrt{\tr((\sigma_0^{-2}I + M J_m)^{-1})}$, and follow the plan with the same LOS guidance. The MPPI planners use~\eqref{eq:prediction} with the respective map (the Fisher-MPPI with~\eqref{eq:fisher} at the beam footprint) and share all other settings. The window and $\sigma_A$ of the AC map were selected among five variants ($w \in \{25, 50, 75\}$~m and $\sigma_A \in \{0.3, 0.5, 1.0\}$~m) on $20$ tuning offsets per terrain at $\sigma_0 = 50$~m, disjoint from the test offsets, and all other parameters were fixed a priori.

\subsubsection{Metrics}
Success, false arrival, and timeout follow Section~\ref{sec:problem}-C. The mean arrival time of successful runs, the mean path length, and the root mean square error (RMSE) of the estimated trajectory are also reported. Paired differences in success are tested with the exact McNemar test, and $95\%$ Wilson confidence intervals (CIs) are given. All simulations were executed on \tbd{[CPU, RAM]}.

\subsection{Overall Performance}
The results over the ten terrains are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:overall}. AC-MPPI attained the highest success rate at every initial uncertainty ($86.7$, $87.3$, and $85.0\%$) and $86.3\%$ over all $900$ runs, and it had the fewest false arrivals and the lowest trajectory RMSE. Its advantage over every planner except Fisher-MPPI was significant ($p < 10^{-4}$). Against Fisher-MPPI, the gap grew with the initial uncertainty ($+0.4$, $+3.3$, and $+4.7$ percentage points) but was not significant at any single $\sigma_0$ ($p = 1.0$, $0.28$, and $0.10$). The baselines fail in different ways. Straight-LOS and the A* planners failed almost exclusively by false arrival, because the PF converged to a wrong hypothesis with a small reported covariance and the vehicle stopped at a wrong location. The MPPI planners also timed out, as they kept steering toward terrain that their maps considered informative while the actual belief failed to contract. The robustness of AC-MPPI comes at the cost of the longest mean path ($2258$~m versus $2147$~m for Fisher-MPPI and $1417$~m for Straight-LOS). On the five terrains on which AC-MPPI succeeded in at least $80\%$ of the runs and at least three baselines in at most $70\%$ (S1, S3, S5, S6, and S7, a selection made after the experiment), AC-MPPI attained $92.0\%$, compared with at most $83.6\%$ for the baselines.

\subsection{Per-Terrain Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:terrain} shows that no baseline is reliable across both kinds of terrain. Fisher-MPPI collapsed on the sand waves of S3 ($45$ of $90$ runs, with $44$ timeouts), where the Fisher map overrates the sand waves (Fig.~\ref{fig:maps}). The Std- and Entropy-MPPI planners collapsed on the stripes of S1 ($56$ runs each, with $34$ false arrivals), the sand waves of S5 ($40$ and $44$ runs, with $31$ and $37$ timeouts), and the slope of S7 ($45$ and $47$ runs), where their maps rate the whole terrain as informative; the Std- and Entropy-A* planners failed on S7 as well ($42$ runs each). AC-A* fell to $43$ runs on S6 and S9, mostly by false arrival, and its success decreased with $\sigma_0$ from $84.7\%$ to $71.3\%$, as a plan computed once from the initial estimate cannot react to a larger start offset. AC-MPPI was within seven runs of the best method on nine of the ten terrains, whereas every baseline fell more than ten runs behind the best on at least three terrains. The exception is S2, where AC-MPPI timed out in $44$ of $90$ runs and was the weakest planner; in a preliminary experiment with $\sigma_A = 0.5$~m and without the normalization, the same planner succeeded in $28$ of $30$ runs on S2 at $\sigma_0 = 50$~m, which suggests that the information scale, set by $\sigma_A$ and one normalization factor per terrain, does not suit every terrain.

The comparison of planners for each map separates the two components of the framework. With the one-shot A* planner, the four maps performed within $2.6$ percentage points of each other ($77.0$--$79.6\%$), so the choice of map matters little when the plan cannot react to the realized belief. With closed-loop MPPI, the directional maps improved ($77.0 \rightarrow 83.6\%$ with Fisher and $78.0 \rightarrow 86.3\%$ with AC), whereas the magnitude-based maps degraded ($79.1 \rightarrow 75.2\%$ with Std and $79.6 \rightarrow 75.1\%$ with Entropy). Closed-loop planning therefore amplifies the quality of the information map: replanning from the current belief repeatedly steers the vehicle toward whatever the map rates as informative, which pays off only if the map discounts ambiguous terrain. With the proposed information, ambiguous terrain carries little information at a large belief size (Remark~\ref{rem:periodic}), so the planner does not count on it until the predicted belief has contracted and instead favors paths whose information is unambiguous at the current belief size.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented an ambiguity-aware active localization framework for TRN of AUVs that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into MPPI control. The information model retains only the information that remains unambiguous at the current belief size, reduces to the Fisher information for small uncertainty, and is precomputed at multiple belief scales for real-time covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model. In 8,100 Monte Carlo runs with a 6-DOF REMUS~100 model on ten real bathymetric maps and three initial uncertainties, AC-MPPI attained the highest success rate at every uncertainty level and the fewest false arrivals, and it was the only planner that remained within seven runs (out of $90$) of the best method on nine of the ten terrains, covering both terrains that mislead the Fisher information and terrains that mislead the standard-deviation and entropy measures. The comparison of planners further showed that closed-loop planning amplifies the quality of the information map, improving the directional maps and degrading the magnitude-based ones. The advantage over Fisher-based MPPI grew with the initial uncertainty but was not statistically significant over the ten terrains, and on one sand-wave field AC-MPPI timed out frequently, which calls for an information scale that adapts to the terrain. This study also assumed a known heading, no currents, and an error-free map, approximated the belief size isotropically, and was conducted in simulation. Future work will address these points and validate the framework in field experiments.
