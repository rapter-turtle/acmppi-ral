%%  ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
Terrain-referenced navigation (TRN) allows autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) to bound dead-reckoning drift by matching altimeter measurements to a bathymetric map, but its reliability depends strongly on the path taken. When the initial uncertainty is large, repetitive or elongated seafloor features such as sand waves and slope bands produce several equally plausible position hypotheses, and the filter may converge to a wrong one. Planners based on the terrain gradient, i.e., the Fisher information, are overconfident on such terrain because the Fisher information is valid only for small uncertainty. This paper presents an ambiguity-aware active localization framework that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into model predictive path integral (MPPI) control. The model evaluates the local terrain autocorrelation under the current belief size, retains only the information that remains unambiguous, and reduces to the Fisher information in the small-uncertainty limit. Precomputed at multiple belief scales, it is embedded in a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model. Monte Carlo simulations with a six-degree-of-freedom REMUS~100 model on eight real bathymetric maps show that the proposed method achieves a mission success rate of 97.5\%, compared with 68.8--92.1\% for straight-line, A*-based, and Fisher-based MPPI baselines and \tbd{XX}\% for a particle-entropy-based MPPI.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} underwater vehicles (AUVs) operate without access to the global navigation satellite system (GNSS), and their dead-reckoning (DR) solution obtained from inertial sensors and a Doppler velocity log (DVL) drifts without bound~\cite{paull2014auv}. Terrain-referenced navigation (TRN) bounds this drift by matching onboard seafloor measurements to a prior bathymetric map~\cite{bergman1999terrain, carreno2010survey, melo2017survey}, even with a single-beam altimeter that most AUVs already carry~\cite{kim2014nonlinear, claus2015terrain, salavasidis2019terrain}, and thereby removes the need to surface for GNSS fixes or to deploy acoustic beacons (Fig.~\ref{fig:auv}).

However, the performance of TRN depends on the terrain the vehicle traverses, and reliable TRN remains challenging due to several coupled factors. First, the initial position uncertainty after a long DR transit can reach tens of meters, which is comparable to the characteristic length of many seafloor features. Second, a single-beam altimeter provides one scalar measurement per ping, so the observable position components depend on the direction in which the terrain is crossed. Third, natural seafloors frequently contain repetitive or elongated features such as sand waves, linear stripes, and slope bands (Fig.~\ref{fig:terrains}), on which several position hypotheses explain the measurements equally well. The particle filter (PF) can then converge to a wrong hypothesis while reporting a small covariance, resulting in a large undetected error at the destination. Therefore, the vehicle must actively select a path that provides information that is not only abundant but also unambiguous at its current level of uncertainty.

Information-aware planners typically quantify the information by the terrain gradient, i.e., the Fisher information, and predict the localization error through the Cram\'{e}r--Rao bound~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, prentice2009belief, hausler2013cooperative}. This prediction is accurate once the belief is narrower than the terrain features, but it is overconfident when the uncertainty is large, because steep but repetitive terrain yields large Fisher information even though its features cannot be told apart. Particle-based information measures avoid this approximation~\cite{hoffmann2010mobile, ryan2010particle}, but their cost grows with the number of particles and simulated measurements, which is prohibitive when hundreds of candidate trajectories must be evaluated at every replanning step.

Motivated by these observations, this paper proposes an ambiguity-aware active localization framework that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into model predictive path integral (MPPI) control~\cite{williams2017model, williams2018information}. The model combines the local terrain autocorrelation with the current belief size and retains only the information that remains unambiguous. Because it depends only on the map and the belief size, it is precomputed at several belief scales and looked up at the cost of a Fisher information map. The MPPI planner embeds the model in a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model, so that information from repetitive features is counted only to the extent that the current belief can resolve them.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An autocorrelation-based terrain information model is proposed, which quantifies the information that remains unambiguous at the current belief size and reduces to the Fisher information in the small-uncertainty limit.
    \item An MPPI-based active localization framework is developed that embeds the information model into a belief-dependent covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model, using multi-scale precomputed information maps for real-time planning.
    \item The framework is validated through Monte Carlo simulations with a six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) REMUS~100 model on eight real bathymetric maps against straight-line, A*-based, Fisher-based, and particle-entropy-based baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
TRN estimates the vehicle position by comparing measured depths or altitudes with a bathymetric map~\cite{bergman1999terrain, nygren2004terrain, melo2017survey}, typically with PFs because the posterior is frequently multimodal~\cite{arulampalam2002tutorial, donovan2012position, teixeira2017robust, kim2018comparison}, and it has been demonstrated with multibeam sonars as well as low-cost altimeters on long-range AUVs and gliders~\cite{meduna2008low, claus2015terrain, salavasidis2019terrain}. These studies treat the path as given and report that the accuracy depends on the terrain traversed~\cite{nygren2004terrain, melo2017survey}. The posterior Cram\'{e}r--Rao lower bound (PCRLB), i.e., the inverse of the accumulated Fisher information of the terrain gradient, is widely used to predict this accuracy~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, bergman1999terrain}, but it characterizes only the asymptotic error around the true position. Large errors caused by competing hypotheses belong to the threshold region, where global bounds such as the Ziv--Zakai bound are governed by the ambiguity of the measurement under a parameter offset rather than by its local curvature~\cite{ziv1969some}. In TRN, this ambiguity is determined by the terrain autocorrelation, which also underlies correlation-based terrain matching~\cite{nygren2004terrain}.

Active localization selects actions that reduce the localization uncertainty~\cite{fox1998active}. Belief-space planners propagate a Gaussian belief with the Fisher information or an extended Kalman filter~\cite{prentice2009belief, vandenberg2011lqg, bry2011rapidly, indelman2015planning}, and perception-aware methods embed Fisher-based localization costs into planning or receding-horizon control~\cite{zhang2019beyond, falanga2018pampc}. For TRN, planners steer the vehicle over terrain with high information content~\cite{hausler2013cooperative}, optimize waypoints for the Monte Carlo probability of reaching the goal~\cite{krukowski2016waypoint}, or select actions that discriminate between PF hypotheses~\cite{fairfield2008active}. Fisher-based predictions inherit the local nature of the Fisher information, whereas particle-based and Monte Carlo evaluations~\cite{hoffmann2010mobile, ryan2010particle, krukowski2016waypoint} are costly inside a sampling-based planner. MPPI control~\cite{williams2016aggressive, williams2017model, williams2018information} optimizes nonsmooth costs over sampled rollouts of nonlinear dynamics and suits active localization if the information cost of each rollout can be evaluated in real time. The proposed framework brings these aspects together: an ambiguity-aware information model that is as inexpensive to evaluate as a Fisher information map, embedded in an MPPI planner with AUV dynamics.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{AUV Model}
The AUV is described by a 6-DOF rigid-body model:
\begin{align}
  \dot{\eta} &= J_\Theta(\eta)\,\nu, \label{eq:kinematics} \\
  M\dot{\nu} + C(\nu)\nu + D(\nu)\nu + g(\eta) &= \tau(\delta_r, \delta_s, n), \label{eq:kinetics}
\end{align}
where $\eta = [x, y, z, \phi, \theta, \psi]^\top$ is the position and attitude, $\nu = [u, v, w, p, q, r]^\top$ is the body-fixed velocity, $J_\Theta$ is the transformation matrix, $M$ is the inertia matrix including the added mass, $C$ is the Coriolis--centripetal matrix, $D$ is the damping including the cross-flow drag and body lift, $g$ is the restoring force, and $\tau$ is generated by the rudder angle $\delta_r$, stern-plane angle $\delta_s$, and propeller speed $n$. The REMUS~100 parameters~\cite{allen2000propulsion} of an open-source implementation\footnote{\url{https://github.com/cybergalactic/PythonVehicleSimulator}} are used. The vehicle cruises at a constant propeller speed, follows the seafloor at a constant altitude with a depth autopilot, and is steered by a heading autopilot.

\subsection{Terrain-Referenced Navigation}
Let $\bp_k = [x_k, y_k]^\top$ be the horizontal position at the $k$-th altimeter ping with period $T_p$. With the heading and attitude measured, the DVL-based DR provides the displacement $\Delta\bp_k = \bp_{k+1} - \bp_k$ as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\hat{\bp}_k = \Delta\bp_k + \mathbf{w}_k, \qquad \mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_w^2 I),
  \label{eq:dr}
\end{equation}
and the altimeter measures the seafloor elevation $h(\cdot)$ of the prior map at its beam footprint,
\begin{equation}
  z_k = h(\bp_k + \bo_k) + v_k, \qquad v_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_z^2),
  \label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
where $\bo_k$ is the footprint offset determined from the attitude and the measured range. A PF with particles $\{\bx_k^i, \omega_k^i\}_{i=1}^{N_p}$~\cite{arulampalam2002tutorial} propagates each particle by $\bx_{k}^i = \bx_{k-1}^i + \Delta\hat{\bp}_{k-1} + \mathbf{q}^i$, $\mathbf{q}^i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_q^2 I)$, and reweights it as
\begin{equation}
  \omega_k^i \propto \omega_{k-1}^i \exp\!\Big( -\beta_k \frac{(z_k - h(\bx_k^i + \bo_k))^2}{2\sigma_L^2} \Big),
  \label{eq:pf}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_L > \sigma_z$ is inflated against weight degeneracy and $\beta_k \in (0,1]$ is the largest tempering exponent that keeps the effective sample size (ESS) above $0.3N_p$. Systematic resampling with roughening is applied when the ESS falls below $0.5N_p$. The PF provides the estimate $\hat{\bx}_k$ and the covariance $P_k$ of the particles.

\subsection{Active Localization Problem}
The vehicle starts at $\bp_0 = \bar{\bp}_0 + \mathbf{e}_0$, $\mathbf{e}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_0^2 I)$, around the nominal start $\bar{\bp}_0$ at which the PF is initialized, and must reach the goal $\bg$. The mission terminates when $\|\bg - \hat{\bx}_k\| \le r_a$. It is successful if $\|\bp_k - \hat{\bx}_k\| \le r_a$ at that moment, i.e., the vehicle has actually reached the goal, a false arrival otherwise, and a timeout if no arrival occurs within $T_{\max}$. The planner seeks heading-rate commands that minimize
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\big[\|\bp_{k_f} - \hat{\bx}_{k_f}\|^2\big] + \alpha\, T_f,
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $k_f$ and $T_f$ are the arrival ping and time and $\alpha$ weights the travel time. Since the belief is multimodal, the expectation is approximated by the trace of the predicted covariance at arrival, which requires predicting how the belief contracts along a candidate path (Section~\ref{sec:info}).

\section{Autocorrelation-Based Terrain Information}
\label{sec:info}

\subsection{Limitation of the Fisher Information}
For the measurement~\eqref{eq:measurement} with the likelihood standard deviation $\sigma_L$, the Fisher information on the position from a single ping at $\bp$ is
\begin{equation}
  \JF(\bp) = \sigma_L^{-2}\,\nabla h(\bp)\,\nabla h(\bp)^\top,
  \label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
and the covariance along a path $\bp_1, \ldots, \bp_T$ is predicted by
\begin{equation}
  P_t = \big( (P_{t-1} + \sigma_q^2 I)^{-1} + J(\bp_t) \big)^{-1}
  \label{eq:riccati}
\end{equation}
with $J = \JF$, which yields the PCRLB~\cite{tichavsky1998posterior, bergman1999terrain}. This prediction is valid only while the error remains in the region where $h$ is approximately linear. On repetitive terrain, positions offset by one period produce identical measurements; if the uncertainty exceeds the period, \eqref{eq:riccati} with $J = \JF$ predicts a contraction that no Bayesian filter can realize (Remark~\ref{rem:periodic}), and a planner relying on it is attracted to precisely the terrain that causes false convergence.

\subsection{Local Autocorrelation and the Offset Posterior}
Consider the expected squared measurement residual when the belief is offset from the truth by $\bd$ while the vehicle crosses a window $\mathcal{W}(\bx)$ of side $w$ centered at $\bx$:
\begin{equation}
  D(\bx, \bd) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\int_{\mathcal{W}(\bx)} \big( h(\bq + \bd) - h(\bq) \big)^2 \, d\bq.
  \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
For locally stationary terrain, $D(\bx,\bd) = 2\big(R_{\bx}(\mathbf{0}) - R_{\bx}(\bd)\big)$ with the local autocorrelation $R_{\bx}$. $D$ vanishes at $\bd = \mathbf{0}$, and a small value at $\bd \neq \mathbf{0}$ indicates an offset that the terrain cannot distinguish from the truth. If $M$ pings are collected within the window while the belief is an isotropic Gaussian of size $\sigma$, the posterior over the offset is
\begin{equation}
  \pi_\sigma(\bd \mid \bx) \propto \exp\!\Big( -\frac{\|\bd\|^2}{2\sigma^2} - \frac{M\,D(\bx,\bd)}{2\sigma_L^2} \Big).
  \label{eq:posterior}
\end{equation}
Its second moment about the true offset,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_\sigma(\bx) = \textstyle\int \bd\,\bd^\top\, \pi_\sigma(\bd \mid \bx)\, d\bd,
  \label{eq:second_moment}
\end{equation}
is the expected squared error after crossing the window, and the autocorrelation-based information per ping is defined as the corresponding increase of the precision,
\begin{equation}
  \JA(\bx; \sigma) = M^{-1}\big[\, \Sigma_\sigma(\bx)^{-1} - \sigma^{-2} I \,\big]_+,
  \label{eq:Jac}
\end{equation}
where $[A]_+ = V\max(\Lambda, 0)V^\top$ for $A = V\Lambda V^\top$. Because $\Sigma_\sigma$ is taken about the true offset rather than the posterior mean, probability mass on a competing offset increases $\Sigma_\sigma$ and reduces $\JA$ in the direction of the ambiguity. The insets of Fig.~\ref{fig:terrains} show $\pi_\sigma$ at the start of each test route.

\subsection{Properties}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fisher}
Let $h$ be four times continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of $\mathcal{W}(\bx)$, and let $G(\bx) = |\mathcal{W}|^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{W}(\bx)} \nabla h\,\nabla h^\top d\bq$. Then, for a fixed $M$, $\JA(\bx;\sigma) = \sigma_L^{-2}\,G(\bx) + O(\sigma^2)$ as $\sigma \to 0$, i.e., $\JA$ converges to the window-averaged Fisher information $\JFbar(\bx)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Taylor expansion, $D(\bx,\bd) = \bd^\top G\bd + c_3(\bd) + O(\|\bd\|^4)$ with a homogeneous cubic form $c_3$. Without the higher-order terms, \eqref{eq:posterior} is a zero-mean Gaussian with precision $A = \sigma^{-2}I + (M/\sigma_L^2)G$, and \eqref{eq:Jac} gives exactly $G/\sigma_L^2$. Since $\pi_\sigma$ concentrates on $\|\bd\| = O(\sigma)$, the first-order perturbation of the second moment by the odd form $c_3$ vanishes, and the remaining perturbations are $\delta\Sigma = O(M\sigma^6) + O(M^2\sigma^8)$. With $\|A\| = O(\sigma^{-2})$, $\Sigma_\sigma^{-1} = A - A\,\delta\Sigma\,A + \ldots = A + O(M\sigma^2)$. Since $G \succeq 0$, the projection $[\cdot]_+$ changes the result by at most the same order, and dividing by $M$ yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:periodic}
For a sand-wave field $h(\bq) = a\cos(2\pi\,\mathbf{n}^\top\bq/\lambda)$ with crest normal $\mathbf{n}$ and a window spanning several wavelengths, $\JFbar = (2\pi a/\lambda)^2/(2\sigma_L^2)\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}^\top$ is independent of $\sigma$, whereas $D(\bx,\bd) = a^2\big(1 - \cos(2\pi\,\mathbf{n}^\top\bd/\lambda)\big)$ vanishes at every $\bd = m\lambda\mathbf{n}$. If $\sigma \gg \lambda$, $\pi_\sigma$ consists of narrow peaks at $m\lambda\mathbf{n}$ weighted by the prior, so that $\Sigma_\sigma \approx \sigma^2 I$ and $\JA \approx 0$; if $\sigma \ll \lambda$, $\JA \approx \JFbar$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher}. Repetitive terrain is thus treated as uninformative until the belief can resolve its period, which is the behavior required for reliable TRN.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Precomputation}
Because~\eqref{eq:D}--\eqref{eq:Jac} depend only on the map and $\sigma$, $\JA$ is precomputed offline on map nodes $\bx$ and belief levels $\mathcal{S} = \{\sigma_1 > \cdots > \sigma_L\}$. For each offset $\bd$ on a square grid with spacing $\Delta_d = \max(\Delta_h, \sigma/5)$ within $\|\bd\| \le 3\sigma$, where $\Delta_h$ is the map resolution, the map $(h(\cdot + \bd) - h(\cdot))^2$ is box-filtered over the window, which yields $D(\cdot,\bd)$ for all nodes at once. The moments~\eqref{eq:second_moment} are accumulated with a streaming log-sum-exp, and $\Delta_d^2/12$ is added to the diagonal to account for the offset-grid resolution. The cost is $O(N_d N_h)$ per level for $N_d$ offsets and $N_h$ map cells. At run time, $\JA(\bx;\sigma)$ is obtained by a nearest-node lookup and a linear interpolation in $\log\sigma$, with $\sigma$ clipped to $[\sigma_L, \sigma_1]$. In this study, $\Delta_h = 5$~m, $w = 50$~m, $M = 33$ (the pings over $50$~m at $1.5$~m/s and $1$~Hz), $\sigma_L = 0.5$~m, $\mathcal{S} = \{50, 35, 20, 10, 5\}$~m, and the nodes are spaced by $20$~m.

\section{Autocorrelation-Informed MPPI}
\label{sec:mppi}
The proposed planner, referred to as the autocorrelation-informed MPPI (AC-MPPI), is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}. At every replanning instant, it samples heading-rate sequences, rolls them out with an AUV maneuvering model from the PF estimate, predicts the covariance at arrival using $\JA$, and passes the optimized command to the heading autopilot.

  \vspace{-14pt}
  \caption{Overall framework. The autocorrelation information maps are precomputed offline from the bathymetric map at multiple belief levels. At each replanning instant, AC-MPPI evaluates sampled heading-rate sequences by rolling out the AUV maneuvering model and predicting the covariance at arrival with the belief-dependent information.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
  \vspace{-12pt}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Rollout Model}
For the rollouts, \eqref{eq:kinetics} is reduced to the horizontal plane ($w = p = q = 0$, $\phi = \theta = 0$, fixed propeller speed), which yields the surge--sway--yaw model
\begin{equation}
  M_h\dot{\nu}_h + C_h(\nu_h)\nu_h + D_h(\nu_h)\nu_h = \tau_h(\delta_r)
  \label{eq:horizontal}
\end{equation}
with $\nu_h = [u, v, r]^\top$ and the corresponding rows and columns of~\eqref{eq:kinetics}. The decision variable is a heading-rate sequence $U = (u_0, \ldots, u_{N-1})$, $|u_n| \le r_{\max}$, each held for $\Delta t$, which the heading autopilot tracks as $\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) = \psi_0 + \int_0^t u\,dt'$ and $r_{\mathrm{ref}} = u$. Each rollout is integrated with a $0.5$~s step and evaluated at its step points $\bp_1, \ldots, \bp_T$ with durations $\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_T$. A rollout that passes within $r_a$ of the goal is terminated there; otherwise, the path is completed by a straight segment to the goal, so that every candidate is evaluated up to arrival.

\subsection{Belief-Dependent Covariance Prediction}
Along each rollout, the covariance is predicted from $P_0 = P$ with $n_t = \tau_t / T_p$ pings per segment as
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:prediction}
\begin{align}
  P_{t|t-1} &= P_{t-1} + n_t\,\sigma_q^2 I, \qquad \sigma_t = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}\tr P_{t|t-1}}, \label{eq:pred_a} \\
  \bar{J}_t &= \textstyle\sum_{j} c_j\, \JA(\bp_t + \bs_j;\, \sigma_t), \label{eq:pred_c} \\
  P_t &= \big( P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + n_t\,\bar{J}_t \big)^{-1}, \label{eq:pred_d}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $\bs_0 = \mathbf{0}$ and $\bs_{\pm i} = \pm\sqrt{3\mu_i}\,\mathbf{e}_i$ are the sigma points of $P_0$ with eigenpairs $(\mu_i, \mathbf{e}_i)$, $c_0 = 1/3$, and $c_{\pm i} = 1/6$. The sigma points average the information over the region where the vehicle may actually be, so that a narrow feature that an offset vehicle would miss does not attract the planner. The belief level $\sigma_t$ is taken from the predicted covariance itself, so repetitive terrain contributes to~\eqref{eq:pred_d} only after the predicted belief has been narrowed by unambiguous terrain earlier along the same rollout. The cost of a sequence $U$ is
\begin{equation}
  S(U) = \tr P_T + \alpha\, T(U) + c_{\mathrm{out}}\,\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{out}}(U),
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $T(U) = \sum_t \tau_t$ and $\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{out}}$ penalizes rollouts that leave the map. Replacing $\JA$ in~\eqref{eq:pred_c} by~\eqref{eq:fisher} at the beam footprint yields the Fisher-based counterpart used as a baseline.

\subsection{MPPI Update}
At each replanning instant, $K$ sequences $U^k = \mathrm{clip}(\bar{U} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k)$, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma_u^2 I)$, are sampled around the nominal sequence $\bar{U}$, which is included unperturbed together with the direct-to-goal sequence $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$. With the weights
\begin{equation}
  \rho^k \propto \exp\!\big(-(S(U^k) - S_{\min})/\gamma\big), \qquad \textstyle\sum_k \rho^k = 1,
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
whose temperature $\gamma$ is set by bisection so that the ESS $(\sum_k (\rho^k)^2)^{-1}$ equals $0.1K$, the nominal sequence is updated as
\begin{equation}
  \bar{U} \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\Big(\bar{U} + \textstyle\sum_{k}\rho^k\,\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^k\Big),
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
and the cheaper of $\bar{U}$ and $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$ is executed. Its first command is applied as $r_{\mathrm{ref}} = u_0$ and $\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) = \psi(t_r) + u_0(t - t_r)$ until the next replanning at $t_r + T_r$, and $\bar{U}$ is shifted by one step as a warm start (Algorithm~\ref{alg:acmppi}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{AC-MPPI at a replanning instant}
\label{alg:acmppi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\hat{\bx}$, $P$, $\psi$, $\nu_h$, $\bg$, $\bar{U}$, precomputed $\JA(\cdot;\sigma)$, $\sigma \in \mathcal{S}$
\STATE Sample $U^k$, $k = 1,\ldots,K$; include $\bar{U}$ and $U_{\mathrm{dir}}$
\FOR{$k = 1$ \TO $K$ \textbf{in parallel}}
  \STATE Roll out~\eqref{eq:horizontal} under $U^k$; append the segment to $\bg$
  \STATE Predict $P_T$ by~\eqref{eq:prediction}; evaluate $S(U^k)$ by~\eqref{eq:cost}
\ENDFOR
\STATE Set $\gamma$ for ESS $= 0.1K$; update $\bar{U}$ by~\eqref{eq:weights}--\eqref{eq:update}
\STATE \textbf{if} $S(U_{\mathrm{dir}}) \le S(\bar{U})$ \textbf{then} $\bar{U} \leftarrow U_{\mathrm{dir}}$
\STATE Apply $u_0$ for $T_r$; shift $\bar{U}$ by one step
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Simulation Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Simulation Setup}
\subsubsection{Vehicle, navigation, and terrains}
The 6-DOF REMUS~100 model was simulated at $10$~Hz with a cruise speed of $1.5$~m/s and an altitude of $8$~m, where the depth set-point was raised for the shallowest map elevation within $200$~m ahead of and $100$~m around the estimate. Navigation ran at $1$~Hz with known heading and without currents (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Eight $2$~km~$\times$~$2$~km crops of the NOAA BlueTopo bathymetry\footnote{\url{https://nauticalcharts.noaa.gov/data/bluetopo.html}} covered by a single survey (source resolution $4$--$16$~m) were resampled to $5$~m (Table~\ref{tab:terrain}, Fig.~\ref{fig:terrains}). One route per terrain was defined by a fixed rule: a length of $1.4$~km ($28\sigma_0$), a heading within $\pm 10^\circ$ of the dominant feature direction obtained from the terrain structure tensor, and the straight corridor with the largest mean expected error after one pass, subject to an informative area within $300$~m. The goal lies in an informative area on five terrains and in an ambiguous area on three. The routes thus represent missions in which the direct path runs along ambiguous structure while unambiguous terrain is reachable; this selection favors information-aware methods over straight-line guidance by design. For each terrain, $30$ initial offsets $\mathbf{e}_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 50^2 I)$ were shared by all methods together with the noise and PF random streams, so that every comparison is paired.

\subsubsection{Compared methods}
1) \emph{Straight-LOS}: line-of-sight (LOS) guidance to the goal ($80$~m lookahead, straight final approach within $150$~m, go-around after a missed arrival). 2) \emph{Fisher-A*}: A* planned once from the initial estimate on an $8$-connected $20$~m grid with the edge cost (length)$\times(1 + \lambda u)$, where $u \in [0,1]$ is the normalized expected error after one pass, $E_{\mathrm{F}} = \sqrt{\tr((\sigma_0^{-2}I + M\JFbar)^{-1})}$, followed by the same LOS guidance. 3) \emph{AC-A*}: the same with $E_{\mathrm{AC}} = \sqrt{\tr\Sigma_{\sigma_0}}$. 4) \emph{Fisher-MPPI}: AC-MPPI with $\JA$ in~\eqref{eq:pred_c} replaced by~\eqref{eq:fisher}. 5) \emph{Entropy-MPPI}: the same MPPI with $\tr P_T$ in~\eqref{eq:cost} replaced by the expected entropy of the particle belief at arrival, estimated from \tbd{$N_e$} subsampled particles and \tbd{$N_z$} simulated measurement sequences per rollout~\cite{hoffmann2010mobile, ryan2010particle}. % TODO: make this description match the implemented baseline.
6) \emph{AC-MPPI} (proposed). Methods 2)--4) and 6) form a factorial design of two information models and two planners. All MPPI variants share $K$, $\Delta t$, $N$, $\sigma_u$, and $\alpha$, which were fixed a priori (see Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}). The A* planners used $\lambda = 8$; with $\lambda = 2$, their pooled success rates were $90.0\%$ (Fisher-A*) and $90.8\%$ (AC-A*).

\subsubsection{Metrics}
Success, false arrival, and timeout follow Section~\ref{sec:problem}-C. The mean arrival time of successful runs, the mean path length, and the root mean square error (RMSE) of the estimated trajectory are also reported. Paired differences in success are tested with the exact McNemar test, and $95\%$ Wilson confidence intervals (CIs) are given. All simulations were executed on \tbd{[CPU, RAM]}.

\subsection{Overall Performance}
The pooled results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:overall}. AC-MPPI succeeded in $234$ of $240$ runs ($97.5\%$), the highest rate among all methods, and its advantage over every baseline was significant in the paired McNemar tests. The failures of the baselines differ in kind. Straight-LOS and the one-shot A* planners failed almost exclusively by false arrival ($75$, $28$, and $19$ runs): the PF converged to a wrong hypothesis with a small reported covariance, and the vehicle stopped at a wrong location. Fisher-MPPI rarely arrived falsely but timed out in $28$ runs, because it kept steering toward terrain that its gradient-based prediction considered informative while the actual belief failed to contract. AC-MPPI reduced both failure types, with $6$ false arrivals and no timeouts, and attained the lowest trajectory RMSE. The improvement costs a longer mission than straight-line guidance ($1204$~s versus $946$~s, $1810$~m versus $1414$~m), whereas, compared with Fisher-MPPI, AC-MPPI traveled a shorter path in a similar time because the ambiguity-aware prediction does not reward loitering over repetitive terrain.

\subsection{Per-Terrain Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:terrain} shows where the improvement originates. On S3, S6, and S7, where unambiguous terrain lies near the route or around the goal, the four A*- and MPPI-based planners succeeded in every run. On S1 and S2, whose elongated features leave only the along-feature offset ambiguous, any path that crosses the stripes or the band resolves the ambiguity; these planners succeeded in at least $29$ of $30$ runs, whereas Straight-LOS, traveling along the features, arrived falsely in $17$ and $5$ runs. The differences concentrate on the sand-wave fields S4, S5, and S8, where the gradient is large but periodic: Fisher-MPPI succeeded in $20$, $12$, and $27$ runs with $28$ timeouts, the one-shot A* planners in $17$--$29$ runs with $47$ false arrivals in total, and AC-MPPI in $28$, $28$, and $29$ runs.

The factorial design separates the two components. Replacing the Fisher information by the proposed information raised the success rate from $88.3\%$ to $92.1\%$ with A* and from $87.1\%$ to $97.5\%$ with MPPI, whereas replacing A* by MPPI changed it from $88.3\%$ to $87.1\%$ with the Fisher information but from $92.1\%$ to $97.5\%$ with the proposed information. Closed-loop planning therefore pays off only in combination with the ambiguity-aware information. With the Fisher information, replanning from the current belief repeatedly predicts that crossing the sand waves will shrink the covariance, so the vehicle keeps returning to the sand-wave field while the actual belief remains multimodal. With the proposed information, the same field carries little information at a large belief size (Remark~\ref{rem:periodic}), so the planner does not count on it until the predicted belief has contracted and instead favors paths whose information is unambiguous at the current belief size. The one-shot A* planners cannot react to the realized belief: a vehicle that misses the planned informative area because of its start offset continues along the plan and arrives falsely.

\subsection{Parameter Sensitivity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
The sensitivity to the time weight $\alpha$ and the horizon $N\Delta t$ was evaluated on S1--S4 with $20$ additional initial offsets per terrain disjoint from the test offsets ($80$ runs per setting, Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}). AC-MPPI attained $95.0$--$100\%$ over the entire grid, whereas Fisher-MPPI ranged from $83.8\%$ to $98.8\%$ and approached the proposed method only with the largest time weight $\alpha = 0.05$. A larger $\alpha$ suppresses the detours that the overconfident Fisher prediction otherwise sustains, which is consistent with its timeout failures. The proposed information model thus removes the need to tune the time weight against the terrain ambiguity.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented an ambiguity-aware active localization framework for TRN of AUVs that integrates an autocorrelation-based terrain information model into MPPI control. The information model retains only the information that remains unambiguous at the current belief size, reduces to the Fisher information for small uncertainty, and is precomputed at multiple belief scales for real-time covariance prediction along rollouts of an AUV maneuvering model. Monte Carlo simulations with a 6-DOF REMUS~100 model on eight real bathymetric maps showed that AC-MPPI achieved a $97.5\%$ success rate, reducing both the false arrivals of straight-line and one-shot A* guidance and the timeouts of Fisher-based MPPI, and that, unlike the Fisher-based planner, its performance was insensitive to the time weight and the horizon on the terrains examined. The factorial comparison further showed that closed-loop planning pays off only when combined with the ambiguity-aware information. This study assumed a known heading, no currents, and an error-free map, approximated the belief size isotropically, and was conducted in simulation. Future work will address anisotropic beliefs and map uncertainty and validate the framework in field experiments.
